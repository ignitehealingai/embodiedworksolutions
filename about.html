About

A real person in your corner. Another member of your team.

Hi, I’m SK. I help people see the bigger picture, figure out what needs to happen next, and then actually do it.

Let’s talk about what you need ↗

Thinking partner. Implementation partner.

People bring me in to figure something out, make a complicated decision, get organized, solve a technology problem, build a business system, learn something unfamiliar, navigate change, or finally finish something they started.

Most project help starts with your calendar. Ours starts with you. First, I pay attention to how you’re feeling and where you’re getting stuck. I take a whole-person approach, drawing on clinical social work training and years of embodiment work, so I may ask about your self-care, your well-being, and the support you have around you. Then we build the plan around all of it. That’s the “embodied” in Embodied Work Solutions.

When a step feels hard, we get curious about it. Maybe it becomes smaller. Maybe we take it together. Maybe someone else is the right person for it.

The common thread

I see how things connect.

My career has taken me through technology, business analysis, project management, organizational change, social work, psychology, coaching, and human-centered design. For a long time I thought those were separate interests. Over time I realized they share a thread.

I’m an intuitive project manager. I notice patterns, dependencies, and what’s quietly getting in the way, and I can hold a lot of threads at once without losing the details. I learn unfamiliar systems quickly, so I don’t need to be an expert in your field to help. You bring what you know; I bring the structure to move it forward.

I love the moment when something that felt impossible becomes manageable, because we finally understand the problem and know what to do first. Sometimes that means designing a better system. Sometimes it means learning a new tool, asking a different question, or figuring out which thing needs to happen first.

My heart is my navigation. It shapes:

  • how I listen
  • how I approach problems
  • how I respond when someone is struggling
  • how I design around real needs
  • how I respect your agency
  • how I keep showing up when it gets complicated

An early example

Early in my career at a public health nonprofit, I noticed colleagues traveling all over North Carolina to give hospitals paper-based assessments. I saw a better way: a web application that could generate customized reports and point people to resources based on their answers.

I worked with a developer to turn the idea into requirements, tested the application, wrote the documentation, and trained the people who used it.

That’s still how I work: see the inefficient process, imagine a better one, bring in the right expertise, and help make it real.

The approach behind the work

Regulation-Centered System Design™

This is the framework underneath everything I do, and the idea at its center is simple: systems work when they align with our nervous systems.

RCSD combines what I know from embodiment and nervous-system work with what I know from systems, project management, and technology.

Every plan, tool, or system we build gets the same test: does it help you stay steady, connected to yourself, and in charge of how you use it? If a tool or app is throwing you off, we find a better option or adjust the one you have until it works for you.

What that means in practice

People first.Tools second.

We choose solutions you can understand and keep using.

I think of my work a bit like the mycelium under these mushrooms: connecting people, ideas, and information below the surface, so what’s above ground can grow.
